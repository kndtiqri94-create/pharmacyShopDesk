## 1. Story
As a **pharmacy cashier, manager or owner using ShopDesk on one or more devices, with or without a working internet connection**, I want **every change I make to be kept safely on my device, sent to the shop's central record automatically and exactly once whenever a connection is available, and to always see honestly whether my work is saved centrally** so that **I can keep selling through any outage, never lose or double-count a sale, never have to think about connectivity, and can see and act on anything the central record could not accept**.

## 2. Business Context
Phase 2 (PRD 5.10, 7.2) made the app work from data on the device (F-3.2) and delivered the central record with its exchange rules (F-3.3). This workstream connects the two: the device now queues every change, sends it in the right order, collects other devices' changes, and tells the user the truth through the status indicator and the Backup & sync settings. Screens, rules and roles do not change; only the previously pretend status indicator and the pretend Sync now / Back up now actions become real.

## 3. Scope
### 3.1 In Scope
- **S-3.4.1 Pending changes and connection awareness.** Every change made on the device (sale, return, stock movement, product edit and so on) is saved together with a record that it is waiting to be sent, as one inseparable step, so a change is never saved without being queued. Waiting changes survive page reload, browser restart and device restart, stay in the order they were made, and are retried automatically after a failure with increasing waiting time between attempts. The app knows at all times whether the device is online or offline.
- **S-3.4.2 Automatic exchange with the central record.** When the connection returns, and then every 60 seconds while online (A46), waiting changes are sent in batches and then the central record's newer changes are collected. Resending the same change, or a whole batch, is safe. When the central record differs on non-stock data, the central version is applied on the device (server wins); stock is always combined from movements. Changes the central record refuses are taken out of the retry cycle and placed in a Needs attention list with the reason. The manual Update Products refresh (S-2.10.10) uses the same exchange rules (OQ48).
- **S-3.4.3 Real status indicator.** The existing status indicator on every screen shows the true state: Online (nothing waiting), Syncing (with count remaining), Offline (with count of waiting changes) and Needs attention (with count of refused changes). It never blocks any screen and replaces the pretend indicator.
- **S-3.4.4 Backup & sync settings made real.** Settings, Backup & sync shows the time of the last successful exchange and the number of waiting changes; Sync now and Back up now work (Back up now means a sync that confirms the central record holds every local change, A50); the automatic-sync toggle really switches background sending on and off; the Needs attention list shows each refused change with its reason; signing out with waiting changes warns the user and asks for confirmation; the limit of local-only data (clearing browser data or losing the device before sending loses unsent changes) is stated in plain words.
- **S-3.4.5 End-to-end acceptance suite.** Automated end-to-end checks covering: a sale made offline, reload and it is still there; one exchange on reconnect with no duplicates; a connection dropped midway through an exchange; two devices selling the same item; the same product price edited on two devices.

### 3.2 Out of Scope (explicit)
- Real sign-in, passwords, sessions and per-role enforcement on the central record (F-3.5); sign-in remains as it is today.
- Hosting, domain, secure certificates, backups of the central database and monitoring (F-3.6); the database backup itself is an operations duty, not a button (A50).
- Any change to screens other than the status indicator and the Backup & sync section of Settings; no change to business rules, roles or permissions.
- Merging two conflicting edits field by field, device-to-device exchange without the central record, and multi-branch exchange (PRD NG3).
- Letting a user edit, fix and resend a refused change from the Needs attention list (the list shows the change and the reason only; handling is a manual, outside-the-app decision unless the Product Owner later asks for more).
- Building or changing the central record itself (delivered in F-3.3).
- Final decisions on OQ47 (what the central record may refuse) and OQ48 (future of manual Update Products), which stay open with the Product Owner; this story follows the working assumptions recorded for F-3.3 (sales are never refused for stock reasons; manual Update Products stays as a full refresh).

## 4. Acceptance Criteria (Given/When/Then)

- **AC-1** — Given a change is made on the device (online or offline), when it is saved, then it is stored and marked as waiting to be sent in one step; there is never a saved change that is not waiting, nor a waiting entry without its change.
- **AC-2** — Given waiting changes, when the page is reloaded, the browser is closed and reopened, or the device is restarted, then every waiting change is still waiting, in the same order, until the central record has accepted it.
- **AC-3** — Given several waiting changes made in a certain order, when they are sent, then the central record receives them in that same order, in batches.
- **AC-4** — Given a send that fails (no response, server error, connection lost), when the app retries, then it waits longer between repeated failures, keeps the changes waiting, and does not lose or reorder any.
- **AC-5** — Given the device loses or regains its connection, when the state changes, then the app knows it is offline or online without the user doing anything, and no screen is blocked or hidden because of it.
- **AC-6** — Given changes waiting and the device comes back online, when the connection returns, then sending starts immediately without a user action.
- **AC-7** — Given the device stays online with automatic sync on, when 60 seconds pass, then waiting changes are sent and the central record's newer changes are collected, with no user action.
- **AC-8** — Given a change was already received by the central record (lost reply, retry, two browser tabs open, or a whole batch resent), when it is sent again, then it is recorded only once and the device treats it as done.
- **AC-9** — Given the central record holds a different version of non-stock data than the device (for example a product price), when the exchange completes, then the device shows the central version.
- **AC-10** — Given two devices each sold the same item while apart, when both exchange with the central record and collect each other's changes, then both sales exist on both devices and the stock equals the starting stock minus both quantities, on each device.
- **AC-11** — Given a sale or return that was completed at the till, when the exchange runs, then that sale or return is never removed from the device by the exchange, even if the central record flags it for review.
- **AC-12** — Given the central record refuses a change, when the answer arrives, then that change stops being retried, is not silently dropped, and appears in the Needs attention list with its reason.
- **AC-13** — Given the manual Update Products refresh is used, when it runs, then it follows the same rules as automatic exchange (server wins, device stock movements sent first) and still works as a full refresh of the product list.
- **AC-14** — Given any screen, when the status indicator is viewed, then it shows Online with nothing pending, Syncing with the count still to send while an exchange is running, Offline with the count of waiting changes when there is no connection, and Needs attention with the count of refused changes when there are any.
- **AC-15** — Given refused changes exist and other changes are waiting, when the indicator is viewed, then Needs attention is shown with its count and it is still possible to see the waiting count in Settings; the indicator never blocks a click, a sale or navigation.
- **AC-16** — Given the previous pretend indicator behaviour, when this workstream is delivered, then the indicator reflects only real connection and queue state and no longer changes on its own or from test actions.
- **AC-17** — Given Settings, Backup & sync, when it is opened, then it shows the time of the last successful exchange (or that none has happened yet) and the number of waiting changes, both updating as exchanges happen.
- **AC-18** — Given waiting changes and a connection, when the user presses Sync now, then waiting changes are sent and server changes collected immediately, the waiting count falls to zero when everything is accepted and the last-sync time updates; if the device is offline, the user is told it cannot sync now and nothing is lost.
- **AC-19** — Given waiting changes and a connection, when the user presses Back up now, then a sync runs and the user is told clearly either that the central record now holds every local change, or what is still outstanding.
- **AC-20** — Given automatic sync is switched off, when changes are made while online, then they stay waiting until Sync now is pressed; when it is switched on again, waiting changes are sent without any press within the 60-second interval. The toggle takes effect instantly without Save changes.
- **AC-21** — Given refused changes exist, when Settings, Backup & sync is opened, then each is listed with what it was, when it was made and the reason it was refused.
- **AC-22** — Given waiting or refused changes exist, when the user signs out, then a clear warning says they are not yet on the central record and may be lost, and signing out proceeds only if the user confirms; with nothing waiting, signing out gives no such warning.
- **AC-23** — Given Settings, Backup & sync, when it is read, then it states in plain words that clearing browser data or losing the device before changes are sent loses those unsent changes.
- **AC-24** — Given a sale is made with the connection off, when the page is reloaded, then the sale is still there and counted once; when the connection returns, it is sent exactly once and no duplicate sale or duplicate stock reduction exists on the central record.
- **AC-25** — Given the connection drops in the middle of an exchange, when it returns, then the exchange resumes, nothing is lost, and nothing is applied twice.
- **AC-26** — Given the same product's price is edited on two devices while apart, when both exchange, then the central record's value is kept on both devices.

## 5. Security & Privacy Notes (business-level only)
- Sensitive data involved: sales and what was bought, customer dues, cash takings, staff pay and personal details, all of which wait on the device until sent.
- Waiting and refused changes must be visible only to a signed-in user of that device, and the Needs attention list and sign-out warning must not expose more than the user already has permission to see in the app.
- Reasons shown for refused changes must be understandable to a shop user and must not reveal technical internals, secrets or other customers' data.
- Passwords are never queued or sent by this workstream.
- Until real sign-in arrives (F-3.5), the existing stop-gap protection of the central record applies; per-role enforcement is not part of this story.
